Plan Options

The right plan depends on your situation. Here’s how we choose.

Every business is different. We analyze your income, age, employee count, and goals to design the plan that produces the most benefit for you specifically.

Solo 401(k)
For self-employed business owners with no employees. Contribute up to $72,000 per year as both employer and employee. One of the most powerful plans available for sole proprietors and single-member LLCs.
Safe Harbor 401(k)
For businesses with employees. Satisfies IRS nondiscrimination testing automatically, allowing owner-employees to max their contributions without restriction. Combined with profit sharing, total annual contributions reach $72,000.
Profit Sharing with Comparability
An add-on to a 401(k) that allows different contribution rates for different groups of employees — legally structured so the owner contributes at a much higher rate than required for staff.
Defined Benefit Plan
For high-earning business owners typically 45 or older who want to contribute far more than 401(k) limits allow. Can stack on top of a 401(k) for combined annual contributions of $200,000–$300,000 or more.
SEP-IRA
A simple, flexible plan with high contribution limits — up to 25% of compensation or $69,000. Easy to administer with no annual testing requirements. Best for business owners who want simplicity without sacrificing limits.
Annual Contribution Optimization
Each year, we review your income, tax situation, and plan performance to optimize contribution amounts. As your business grows, your plan strategy should evolve with it.

Common Questions

Questions I get asked most

What retirement plan should a small business owner choose?

It depends on whether you have employees and how much you want to contribute. A business with no employees other than the owner and spouse usually gets the most from a Solo 401(k). A business with employees that wants meaningful owner contributions typically needs a Safe Harbor 401(k) with profit sharing. A SIMPLE IRA fits businesses prioritizing low cost and low administration.

How much can a business owner put into their own 401(k)?

In 2026, total annual additions to one person's 401(k) account are capped at $72,000, or $80,000 at age 50 and older. The owner's own salary deferral tops out at $24,500, so reaching the full amount requires employer match and profit sharing contributions, which is determined by plan design.

What is a Safe Harbor 401(k) and do I need one?

A Safe Harbor 401(k) requires the employer to make a set contribution, commonly a 4% match or 3% to all eligible employees, and in exchange the plan automatically passes annual nondiscrimination testing. Owners who have had deferrals refunded after failed testing generally need it. New Safe Harbor plans must generally be effective by October 1.
