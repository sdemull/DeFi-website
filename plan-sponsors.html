Common Questions

Questions I get asked most

What are my fiduciary responsibilities as a 401(k) plan sponsor?

If your business sponsors a retirement plan, you are a fiduciary under ERISA. That means you must act solely in the interest of plan participants, select and monitor investments prudently, ensure fees are reasonable, and document the process you used. Fiduciary liability is personal, and it applies whether or not anyone explained it to you when the plan was set up.

How often should a 401(k) plan be reviewed?

Investment lineups and fees should be reviewed at least annually, with the review documented in writing. Plan design should be revisited whenever the business changes materially, such as significant growth in payroll or headcount. Many small business plans go years without a documented review, which is itself a fiduciary exposure.

Can I be held personally liable for problems with our company 401(k)?

Yes. ERISA fiduciary liability extends to personal assets, which surprises most small business owners. Common exposures include failing to monitor investment options, paying unreasonable fees, and not following the terms of the plan document. Working with a fiduciary advisor and documenting decisions substantially reduces that risk.
