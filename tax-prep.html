How We Approach Tax

Three levels. Most advisors only work at one.

Tax prep, tax planning, and financial planning are connected. When all three are aligned, your tax return stops being a report card and starts being a roadmap.

1
Level 1 — Foundation
Tax Preparation
An accurate, on-time return is the starting point. We prepare federal and state returns for business owners, the self-employed, and individuals with complex situations — S-Corps, partnerships, rental income, investment accounts, and more. But a return filed correctly is just the beginning.
Individual (1040) and business returns (1120-S, 1065, Schedule C)
Self-employment and S-Corp owner returns
Rental income, investment accounts, and capital gains
Coordination with bookkeeper or payroll provider
2
Level 2 — Strategy
Tax Planning
Tax planning is what happens between returns. It's the proactive decisions made throughout the year that change what your next return looks like. For business owners especially, this is where the real money is. The gap between reactive tax filing and proactive tax planning is often $20,000–$50,000 per year — sometimes more.
Year-round tax projection — we know your number before December
S-Corp election analysis and implementation
Retirement plan contributions timed to maximize deductions
Roth conversion windows identified and executed
QBI deduction optimization for pass-through entities
Capital gain and loss timing in investment accounts
3
Level 3 — Integration
Financial Planning
This is where tax work stops being a line item and starts being a wealth-building strategy. When your tax plan is wired into your financial plan, every dollar saved in taxes gets redirected with intention — into retirement accounts, investments, or debt reduction. Tax decisions become financial decisions.
Tax savings routed directly into retirement plan contributions
Investment accounts managed with tax efficiency as a core goal
Business exit structured to minimize the tax hit on the sale
Estate planning coordinated to reduce transfer taxes
Every major financial decision evaluated through a tax lens first

Year-Round Approach

Tax season is 12 months long.

The return you file in April is the result of decisions made all year long. Here’s how we stay ahead of it every month — and how that connects to your overall financial plan.

Jan – Mar
Return Preparation & Filing
Gather documents, prepare and file returns accurately. Review prior year for missed opportunities.
Apr – Jun
Entity & Compensation Review
Assess S-Corp election timing, set reasonable salary, review Q1 estimated taxes. Identify this year’s biggest opportunities.
Jul – Sep
Mid-Year Tax Projection
Run a full projection of this year’s tax liability. Adjust withholding or estimates. Identify retirement contribution runway remaining.
Oct – Nov
Year-End Planning Window
Execute Roth conversions, harvest losses, make final retirement contributions. Every major move needs to happen before December 31st.
December
Final Execution & Review
Confirm all moves are complete. Final estimated payment if needed. Set up next year’s strategy before the calendar turns.
