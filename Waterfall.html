Assumptions guide
Filing situation Suggested rate What to consider
Married filing jointly, moderate income 18–22% Typical blended rate when income includes qualified dividends and long-term capital gains, which are taxed at 0–15% federally. Michigan adds a flat 4.25% state tax.
Married filing jointly, higher income 22–28% Once taxable income crosses ~$190K (MFJ), the 22% federal bracket applies. Add Medicare surtax (3.8%) on net investment income above $250K. State tax unchanged.
Single filer 24–30% Lower bracket thresholds mean single filers reach higher brackets sooner. A single retiree drawing $180K gross would likely land in the 22–24% federal bracket plus MI state.
Heavy RMD / pre-tax accounts 26–35% If most assets are in traditional IRAs or 401(k)s, all distributions are ordinary income. RMDs at age 73+ can push income into higher brackets and trigger IRMAA surcharges on Medicare premiums.
Roth-heavy / tax-efficient portfolio 10–16% Roth distributions are tax-free. If most income flows from Roth accounts or stepped-up basis taxable accounts, effective rates can be very low. A lower rate here means a smaller gross income need.
Business owner / self-employed income 28–40% Active business income adds self-employment tax (15.3% on first ~$170K). Use a higher rate if any retirement income will still carry SE exposure, or if distributions from an S-corp are involved.
Michigan note: Michigan taxes retirement income, but residents born before 1946 may deduct pension income; those born 1946–1952 have partial exemptions phasing in through 2026. Ask your advisor whether the Michigan pension subtraction applies to your situation — it can materially reduce your effective rate.
Bucket / asset class Conservative Current environment context
Bucket 1 HYSA / cash 2.5–4.5% As of mid-2025, competitive HYSAs and money market funds yield roughly 4–5%. However, the Fed has begun cutting rates — conservative planning should assume yields drift toward 3% over the bucket's 3-year life. Use 3.0–3.5% as a realistic forward estimate.
Bucket 2 Fixed income 4.0–6.0% Intermediate-term investment-grade bonds (3–7 year duration) currently yield 4.5–5.5%. Locking in a CD ladder or bond ladder for years 4–8 at today's rates is prudent. Use 5.0–5.5% if laddering now; 4.5% if assuming lower future reinvestment rates.
Bucket 3 Balanced / income 5.5–7.5% A balanced portfolio of 50–60% equities and 40–50% bonds + alternatives has historically returned 6–8% annualized over 10-year windows. Use 6.5–7.0% as a planning baseline. REITs and dividend equities add income but can be volatile in rate-sensitive environments.
Bucket 4 Market index 7.0–10.0% The S&P 500 has averaged ~10% nominally and ~7% real over long historical periods. Many financial planners use 7–8% for conservative long-range projections (accounting for valuation risk and lower expected returns vs. historical). Use 9–10% only as an optimistic scenario.
Weighted average (blended) 6.0–8.0% A well-structured 4-bucket portfolio with $10M and the defaults above produces a blended return of roughly 7–8%. The weighted average updates live above as you adjust return inputs — watch how Bucket 4's weight dramatically influences the portfolio-level number.
Historical context: The 60/40 portfolio (the traditional proxy for balanced investing) returned approximately 10.3% annualized over 1926–2023, with significantly more volatility than any single bucket shown here. The bucket strategy's purpose is not to maximize return — it is to protect spending in early years while allowing growth to compound in later buckets without forced liquidation during downturns.
Scenario Rate to use Rationale
Fed target / normalized 2.0–2.5% The Federal Reserve's long-run inflation target is 2%. If you believe inflation will normalize to this level over the planning horizon, this is the appropriate input. It implies your purchasing power erodes slowly and Bucket 1 needs less capital.
Baseline / moderate 3.0% The default used in this tool. Reflects the 30-year historical average for CPI-U (roughly 2.8–3.2%). Appropriate for most planning scenarios — slightly conservative vs. the Fed target, which builds in a small buffer for unexpected cost increases.
Healthcare-heavy spending 3.5–4.5% Medical inflation (MCPI) has historically run 1–2% above general CPI. If healthcare is a significant portion of retirement spending — which it typically becomes after age 75 — use a higher assumption to avoid underestimating future income needs.
Elevated / stressed scenario 4.5–6.0% Use for stress testing only. The 2021–2023 inflation spike peaked near 9% CPI before normalizing. Running this scenario shows how much additional capital Bucket 1 would need if sustained higher inflation returns — useful for identifying downside risk.
Why inflation matters here: Inflation affects this model in two ways. First, it increases the gross income needed each year — each Bucket 1 year is restated in inflation-adjusted dollars. Second, the "real" return of each bucket is its assumed nominal return minus inflation. A 5.5% bond return with 4% inflation is only 1.5% real. The tool uses nominal returns throughout; keep this in mind when evaluating whether projected bucket growth truly outpaces your cost of living.

Projections assume constant nominal returns and do not account for taxes on investment gains, sequence-of-returns risk, or changing income needs. Gross income need = net ÷ (1 − effective tax rate). Bucket 1 is sized to cover inflation-adjusted gross income for years 1–3 in cash. Buckets 2 and 3 are present-valued to today at their respective assumed returns.
